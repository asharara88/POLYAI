# Pack: `rak-media`

Industry pack for a government-mandated multi-surface media operator in the UAE evolving from institutional comms into a commercial IP + partnership platform. Modelled on RAK Media's position ahead of Wynn Al Marjan (guided Sep 2027) and the 2028 hotel wave, with ADM and DMI as structural benchmarks.

## When this pack applies

- Government-mandated media office or media authority with legal personality (e.g. established under an emirate-level law reporting to a Ruler)
- Multi-surface audience footprint: radio, social, YouTube, podcast — one operator, many platforms
- Transitioning from a pure communications function to a commercial media operation (partnerships, sponsorship, IP franchises) without surrendering the institutional mandate
- Serves a specific emirate or geography as its "home cell" while addressing national and GCC advertisers

## Audience segments

RAK Media's audience is domestic-and-resident-led today; international arrivals are scheduled, not assumed. Six segments shape every brief:

1. **Residents** — long-term UAE residents in the home emirate (RAK: ~450,000)
2. **Visitors** — weekenders, GCC visitors, domestic tourism
3. **Newcomers** — people considering a move, families relocating (Heart of RAK platform targets this cohort)
4. **Workforce** — the people building the destination (hotel operators, construction, services)
5. **Business leaders** — investors, founders, operators of the emirate's commercial scene
6. **Government stakeholders** — the Ruler's Court, federal counterparts, the GSEC, visiting delegations

The questions each segment asks are qualitative signals from relocation guides and community discussion — not polling. The core job of the media operation is to answer those questions systematically across the four surfaces.

## The four surfaces (one engine, one calendar, one rights framework)

- **Radio** — the daily trusted presence; multilingual (Arabic, English, Malayalam, Hindi, Tagalog); live events, drive-time, daily segments
- **Social** — discovery layer; Instagram, TikTok, X, LinkedIn; short-form, newsworthy, shareable
- **YouTube** — searchable archive and the long-form home; franchise series, documentaries, interviews
- **Podcast** — repurposes the best conversations; weekly rhythm; destination for depth and dialogue

The surfaces are not four organisations. One engine runs them: one calendar, one rights framework, one audience data pipeline.

## Five revenue lines (ranked by strategic order, not volume)

1. **Partnerships** — category-exclusive Official Partner positions for the opening year of major moments (Wynn, hotel wave). Modelled on Expo 2020's partner structure. **New money**, so these lead. Position bands are proposed prices against the buyer's filed marketing spend; they become numbers after the first two positions are negotiated.
2. **Sponsorship** — title sponsors on franchises (Destination RAK, Jebel Jais, Heart of RAK) and tentpole events (RAK Half Marathon, Global Media Congress tie-ins).
3. **Franchises** — three named IP franchises, each extended across all four surfaces; advertiser-funded series following the Majid pattern (one IP extended across radio + social + YouTube + podcast).
4. **Spot advertising** — fourth by design, hard-ceiling. RAK FM inventory + published Dubai rates cap this at AED 4-13m. Not where the growth is.
5. **Production services** — small in-house unit selling content, event coverage, and documentary production to third parties when it does not conflict with the primary mission.

**Packaging**: Four tiers from Official Partner → Sponsor → Franchise → Spot. Value-in-kind permitted up to 20% on the top two tiers.

## Who buys — four tiers of accounts

1. **Home accounts in RAK** — RAKBANK, RAK Properties, RAK Ceramics, Al Hamra Group, Julphar, Stevin Rock, Marjan, RAKEZ, RAK FTZ, RAKTDA
2. **National brands with a RAK footprint** — Abu Dhabi and Dubai HQs with operations or interests in RAK (Etihad Rail, Emirates NBD, Mashreq, major hotel operators pre-opening)
3. **National advertisers** — brands who buy the Northern Emirates once the measurement cell exists (property, telecoms, retail, banks — the four categories that fund the UAE market)
4. **Partnerships** — counterparties who open doors rather than write cheques directly (ADM, DMI, federal entities, international broadcasters, Expo-style strategic partners)

The first twenty named accounts are sequenced: RAKBANK first, the hotel wave last. Eighty names behind the twenty make the funnel work.

## By-emirate geography of buyers

- **RAK** — home accounts; direct relationship, in-house ownership
- **Umm Al Quwain + Ajman** — catchment inside the measurement cell; home-adjacent
- **Sharjah + Dubai** — national brands buying through a Dubai desk
- **Abu Dhabi** — both buyers (national brands) and partners (ADM, federal entities)
- **Fujairah** — partnership only, on the other side of the Hajar mountains

## Funnel economics

Benchmark (single published sponsorship-sales example, used as-is until discovery produces actuals):
- 30% of named accounts take a meeting
- One-third of meetings produce a proposal
- 50% of proposals close
- → 80 names ≈ 4 accounts
- → 20-account book needs ~400 names at those rates

Six Official Partner positions is a **product limit** (one per category), not a conversion result. The target preset in commercial planning shows the conversion rates eighty names would need to produce twenty — labelled as a gap-to-close, not a forecast.

## KPI framework

**Audience / currency**
- Measured cell size (home emirate, measured monthly, PwC-verified or equivalent third-party)
- Reach by surface + unduplicated reach across the four surfaces
- Audience quality: residents vs visitors vs newcomers split

**Commercial pipeline**
- Named accounts in motion (of 80 target)
- Conversion rates by stage (meeting / proposal / close) vs benchmark
- Weighted pipeline value by revenue line
- Addressable spend coverage (% of filed FY marketing spend of target accounts touched)

**Revenue (reported quarterly)**
- Revenue by line (Partnerships / Sponsorship / Franchise / Spot / Production) — Partnerships leads
- Card-rate discipline: % of deals at card vs discounted
- Value-in-kind % (ceiling: 20% on top two tiers)
- Partnership renewal rate (year 2+ of Official Partner relationships)

**Operations**
- Commercial opex envelope: AED 3-6m/yr floor for the three priced lines
- Content throughput by franchise × surface
- Government stakeholder NPS (quarterly, qualitative)

- **2028 hotel wave** — multiple branded operators opening across Marjan Island

## Compliance flags

Every externally-facing artifact for a `rak-media` client must respect these constraints:

- **Institutional mandate** — the operator reports to the Ruler; the editorial line reflects emirate policy
- **UAE Media Regulatory Office (UAE Media Council) licensing** — content classification, advertising standards, political-content rules
- **Ruler's Court protocol** — Ruler's media artifacts route through the Ruler's own channel; no commercial sponsorship within those
- **Government-stakeholder discretion** — ministerial / royal references require stakeholder-manager sign-off
- **Partnership exclusivity** — Official Partner positions are category-exclusive; conflicting commitments are a contract breach

## Operating rules (each answers a published Dubai/Abu Dhabi mistake)

1. **Trade behind a currency, never ahead of one.** The market traded on vendor telephone surveys until PwC-verified Nielsen landed in 2017; do not sell what cannot be measured.
2. **Never discount the card.** Discounting resets the market for every subsequent conversation. Value-in-kind on the top two tiers, not price.
3. **Keep relationships in-house.** ADM leased its agency trading to Media Sat (Choueiri) in Feb 2021 and brought it back in Feb 2022 citing speed; DMI (represented by MEMS/Choueiri for two decades) took sales in-house from Jan 2026. Both read the same lesson. Keep them.
4. **Sell the catalyst before it opens.** Wynn's budget decisions are made 12-18 months before launch. Build before the moment; audience, relationships, CRM and data compound and cannot be bought in the quarter the doors open.

## Channel strategy (how commercial sells)

- **In-house for relationships** — the twenty named accounts
- **Dubai desk for volume** — national-brand media buying out of Dubai
- **Platform partner for programmatic** — operator for digital inventory not sold directly
- **Broker only on success fee** — never on retainer
- **Agencies briefed, not relied on** — PHD plans Wynn's media from Dubai; brief them, do not outsource the relationship

## Voice notes

- **Institutional, not corporate.** The operator carries the emirate's voice; it does not perform brand voice.
- **Multilingual, Arabic-first.** Arabic leads; English, Malayalam, Hindi, Tagalog follow depending on surface and audience segment.
- **Trusted, not promotional.** Daily trusted presence (radio) is the asset. Promotional voice damages it.
- **Long-term, not launch-cycle.** The operator exists before every launch and will exist after every launch.
- **Discrete on protocol.** Royal and ministerial references are matter-of-fact, never performative.

## Common pitfalls

- **Treating this as pure private-sector ad sales.** The government mandate still constrains what can be commercialised; rights-licensing-coordinator gates every commercial package.
- **Chasing programmatic volume for revenue.** The spot ceiling (AED 4-13m) is a mathematical cap, not an aspiration. Partnerships lead.
- **Copying ADM/DMI without reading their lessons.** The structures matter less than why they were restructured; see operating rules.
- **Running the four surfaces as four organisations.** One engine, one calendar, one rights framework; otherwise franchises fragment.
- **Selling Wynn as the strategy.** Wynn is a catalyst; the strategy is the audience, IP and CRM that compound across every launch.
