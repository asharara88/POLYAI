## Tier 3 — National advertisers (buy on measured cell)

Buy the Northern Emirates when the currency makes it individually sellable. These accounts do not buy today; they buy post-measurement.

Property (property developer category), Telecoms (Etisalat / du), Retail (national retailers), Banking (the national banks above also fit here when the cell is national not RAK-home). The planner's argument: PHD plans Wynn's media from Dubai; the operator does not compete on reach, it offers the only platform that sells the home-cell as a measured unit.

## Tier 4 — Partnerships (open doors, not buy)

Not advertisers. Counterparties who make the operator stronger: ADM, DMI, federal entities (UAE Media Council, NMO, WAM, Office of Public and Cultural Diplomacy), international broadcasters (DW, BBC, Al Jazeera English), Expo-style strategic partner templates.

## Buyer attributes shared across tiers

- UAE marketing + comms heads at CMO or Head-of-Media level
- Decision horizon: quarters (home accounts) to 12-18 months (Wynn-adjacent partnerships)
- Buying through a Dubai agency on volume (PHD, OMD, GroupM, Dentsu, Starcom)
- Measurement-sensitive — a published, third-party-audited cell changes everything
- Protocol-aware — government-mandated operator carries weight they do not get from private media

## What the operator refuses

- Buyers who want Ruler-touching content with commercial attachment
- Buyers who insist on below-card pricing
- Buyers who demand editorial control of a franchise

## Lead-source priorities (quarter 1 of the run-in)

1. Named-account direct outreach (first 20)
2. ADM / DMI commercial-director referrals (counterparty partnerships)
3. UAE agency briefings (brief the planner, not the client — then let the planner recommend)
4. Trade press and Global Media Congress / BRIDGE Summit relationship-making
